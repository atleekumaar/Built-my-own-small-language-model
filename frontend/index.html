Why Perplexity Cannot Be Compared Across Tokenizers: Perplexity is defined as \(PPL = \exp(CE)\) where Cross-Entropy is normalized per token. Because word tokens span ~6.3 bytes/token while BPE subwords span ~3.5 bytes/token, each BPE token represents a smaller slice of entropy. Furthermore, the Word model naively maps unseen words to <UNK> without predicting their constituent characters (ignoring ~15.6 bits of entropy per unseen word). Always use Bits-per-Byte (BPB) for scientifically valid cross-architecture comparisons.

Logits & Temperature Scaling

The model outputs raw unnormalized logits \(z \in \mathbb{R}^V\). Scaled Softmax converts logits into normalized next-token probabilities:
P(w_i) = exp(z_i / T) / ∑_j exp(z_j / T) When \(T \to 0\), the distribution sharpens toward greedy argmax. As \(T \to \infty\), it flattens toward uniform random sampling.

Phase 5 Scientific Validation & Fair Model Comparison

Rigorous evaluation on identical 47 held-out validation paragraphs (15,916 UTF-8 bytes) with byte-normalized likelihood (BPB)

Scientific Methodology Warning — Cross-Tokenizer Perplexity Comparability: Per-token perplexity (PPL = exp(CE)) cannot be compared directly between Word and BPE models because their token granularities differ (6.3 bytes/token for Word vs. 3.5 bytes/token for BPE). Furthermore, the Word model's raw loss naively maps 22.7% of held-out tokens to <UNK> without modeling the unseen characters. To establish a fair benchmark, we evaluate Bits Per UTF-8 Byte (BPB) on identical text, both raw and adjusted for open-vocabulary unseen word entropy.
